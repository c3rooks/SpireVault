Co‑op

Find someone to climb with.

Slay the Spire 2 co‑op goes through Steam friends, so you have to know someone before you can play with them. SpireVault matches you with whoever else is looking. The game itself still starts through Steam.

Three acts. Easy.

  1. 1

    Sign in with Steam.

    Official Steam OpenID. You type your password on Steam's page, never ours.

  2. 2

    Tap Play.

    Pick a climber, or Any, and your game version. If someone on your version is already waiting, you're matched at once.

  3. 3

    Nobody around? Get pinged.

    Turn pings on and you can close the tab. Your browser pings you when a player is looking; tap it to come back and play. If it can't ping, keep the tab open and listen for the chime.

  4. 4

    Add each other, then climb.

    The game only lets Steam friends join, so the party card gives everyone an Add button and a Launch button. Up to four players. Whoever waited longest hosts.

Privacy

Your saves stay on your machine.

SpireVault only reads the run files the game already wrote. It isn't a mod, and every line of it is on GitHub.

Stays with you

  • Your raw save files. SpireVault reads them and never uploads them.
  • Your Steam password. Sign-in happens on Steam's own page.
  • Your Steam friends list. SpireVault never reads it.

Goes to the server, only after you sign in with Steam

  • Your Steam ID, name and avatar, when you were last active, and whether Steam shows you in Slay the Spire 2. Other signed-in players can see these.
  • Your co‑op pick (climber and game version), a log of when you played, and, if you turn pings on, your browser's notification address.
  • A copy of your run history, so your other devices have it.
  • A session that ends within 30 days of your last visit. Signing out ends it at once and removes your ping address. Your run copy stays.

Signed in or not, the app also sends anonymous usage events (which tab you open, how many files you import, your browser type) to the SpireVault server and to Google Analytics.

FAQ

Questions.

Is SpireVault affiliated with Mega Crit?

No. It's an unofficial, fan-made companion. Mega Crit owns Slay the Spire. SpireVault doesn't host games, route invites or replace anything they built.

Is it a mod? Could it get me banned?

No, it's not a mod. It doesn't change the game, inject into the game process, load DLLs or read game memory. It only reads the .run files Slay the Spire 2 writes to your save folder after each run, the same files you could open in a text editor.

Do I need an account?

Not for stats. Co‑op needs a Steam sign-in, which happens on Steam's page. Sign out any time and the session is gone.

What does it cost?

$0. No tier, no premium, no supporter perks. I cover hosting (a few dollars a month) and the $14 a year domain. It's MIT licensed, so even if I disappear, anyone can fork it and keep it running.

Do new runs show up on their own?

Where the browser lets a page keep reading a folder, SpireVault re-reads yours in the background. In most browsers it can't, so click Refresh and choose the folder again.

Do my runs sync between devices?

Only if you sign in with Steam. Then a copy of your run history is kept under your Steam ID, so a new device or a clean install gets it back. Without signing in, your runs stay on this device.

Does SpireVault track me?

A little, and never your save files. Signed in or not, the app sends anonymous usage events (which tab you open, how many files you import, your browser type) to the SpireVault server and to Google Analytics. Signing in adds what the privacy section lists, and nothing more.

How does co‑op work?

Sign in with Steam, pick a climber and your game version (Stable or Beta), and tap Play. You're matched with whoever is waiting on the same version. If nobody is, you wait in line. A party holds up to four, and the player who waited longest hosts. The game only lets Steam friends join, so the party card has an Add button for each player. The run itself still starts in Steam.

Do I have to keep the tab open while I wait?

Not if your browser can send notifications. Turn pings on and SpireVault pings you when a player is looking, even with the tab closed. Where a browser can't, keep the tab open and you'll hear a chime.

What is Run Coach (beta)?

An opt-in floating panel in the macOS and Windows apps. Ask a question and it sends that, your current run (character, HP, gold, deck, relics) and a screenshot of the game to OpenAI or Anthropic, using your own API key. Run Coach talks straight to the provider: SpireVault's server never sees your question, your screenshot or your key, and nothing is billed by me. It doesn't touch the game. On macOS it is hidden from screen recordings by default; the Windows app does not hide it yet. How it works.

Is the Windows app the same as the macOS app?

Same app. Both open app.spirevault.app in their own window. The Windows installer is about 2 MB because it uses the WebView2 that ships with Windows 11. It is unsigned, so SmartScreen asks once.

Can someone impersonate me?

No. Anything saved under your Steam ID needs a session that is only issued after Valve has verified your Steam sign-in. The details are in SECURITY.md.

Why should I trust this with my Steam account?

You don't have to. Your password goes to Steam; the server only gets the Steam ID that Valve hands back. The whole stack is on GitHub. Read it, build it yourself, file an issue if something looks wrong.

Can I host my own copy?

Yes. This site is static files, the web app adds a small Pages proxy, and the server is one Cloudflare Worker with a KV namespace and a Durable Object. The repo's Makefile deploys all three with wrangler. Bring your own Steam Web API key, plus VAPID keys if you want pings.

What's the new look?

A redesign of the app, in beta and off by default. Open app.spirevault.app/?ui=next to try it and ?ui=classic to go back. Same runs, same stats, no re-import.

Is there a phone version?

The web app works on a phone. There's also Ascension Companion, my separate run tracker for iPhone and iPad, on the App Store.
